## 3. Scientific & Evolutionary Perspectives  
1. **Biological Purpose**: From a purely evolutionary standpoint, the “goal” of any living organism is to **survive and reproduce**.  
2. **Cosmic Context**: In cosmology, life is a rare, emergent phenomenon in an otherwise indifferent universe—a byproduct of the laws of physics.  
3. **Consciousness & Narrative**: Neuroscience suggests that the brain constructs a **narrative self**; meaning is a mental model that helps us predict, plan, and cooperate.  
4. **Collective Projects**: Many scientists argue that meaning emerges when individuals contribute to *knowledge* or *culture* that outlasts their own lifespans (e.g., art, science, institutions).

### Takeaway
The scientific view doesn’t prescribe a *purpose*; it describes the **conditions** under which meaning can arise (social bonding, storytelling, legacy).

## 4. Psychological Findings on What Makes Life Feel Meaningful  
- **Purposeful Engagement**: Activities that align with personal values and challenge you enough to promote growth (flow states).  
- **Connection**: Deep relationships—family, friends, community—are the strongest predictors of perceived meaning.  
- **Contribution**: Feeling that you’re making a positive impact on others or on a cause larger than yourself.  
- **Coherence**: Understanding your life story as a coherent narrative (past, present, future).  
- **Transcendence**: Moments of awe, spirituality, or feeling part of something vast.

> **Study highlight**: A 2022 meta‑analysis of 80+ longitudinal studies (Steger & Wong) found that purpose predicts better mental health, longevity, and resilience **independently** of happiness.
