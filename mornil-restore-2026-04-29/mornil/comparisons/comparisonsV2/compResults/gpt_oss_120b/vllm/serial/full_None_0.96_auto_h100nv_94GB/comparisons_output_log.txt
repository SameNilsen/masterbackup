| Feature | Typical Range | Notable Record |
|---------|----------------|----------------|
| **Length** | 24–30 m (78–98 ft) | A 33 m (108 ft) individual was measured in 1909 (though modern verified records top out around 30 m). |
| **Weight** | 100–150 t (220,000–330,000 lb) | The heaviest recorded blue whale weighed about 190 t (420,000 lb). |

### Why the blue whale is so massive
- **Filter‑feeding lifestyle:** Blue whales consume up to 4 t (8,800 lb) of krill per day during feeding seasons. Their enormous size allows them to process huge volumes of water and capture massive amounts of prey efficiently.
- **Aquatic buoyancy:** Living in water eliminates the need for a skeletal structure capable of supporting such mass against gravity, allowing evolution toward gigantic dimensions.
- **Evolutionary history:** Whales evolved from land mammals about 50 million years ago, gradually increasing in size as they adapted to marine life and exploited abundant planktonic food sources.

### Fun facts
- **Vocalizations:** Their low‑frequency calls can travel across entire ocean basins (up to 1,000 km) and are among the loudest animal sounds on Earth (up to 188 dB re 1 µPa at 1 m).
- **Migration:** Blue whales undertake some of the longest migrations of any mammal, traveling 10,000–16,000 km (6,200–10,000 mi) between high‑latitude feeding grounds and low‑latitude breeding grounds each year.
- **Conservation status:** Listed as *Endangered* by the IUCN. Historic whaling reduced populations by ~90 %; today, numbers are slowly recovering but remain far below pre‑whaling levels.

So, when you think of the biggest mammal, picture a creature as long as a basketball court, weighing as much as 30 adult African elephants combined, and moving gracefully through the world’s oceans—that’s the blue whale.
